Five jobs arrive at one CPU. The first, P1, requires eight time units; the others require one to three. Each figure shows a real schedule under its policy, with one row per job. Solid bars show CPU time, and hatching shows time spent waiting after arrival. All three use the same time axis.

<FcfsSchedule />

FCFS runs jobs to completion in arrival order. Other jobs wait for P1 to finish: a long job holding up shorter followers is the convoy effect.

<SjfSchedule />

SJF chooses the shortest waiting job whenever the CPU becomes free. This example is non-preemptive, so P1 still runs to completion, but the changed order afterwards reduces mean waiting time.

<RoundRobinSchedule />

Round robin runs each job for at most a fixed quantum, then places unfinished work at the back of the queue. Short jobs can progress before a long job finishes, with additional context-switch overhead.
