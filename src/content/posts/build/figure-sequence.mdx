Read a sequence diagram from top to bottom to see who sent what to whom. The `sequence` kind adds travel time: an arrow slopes from its send time to its arrival time, with a packet moving along it.

This example establishes a TCP connection with a three-way handshake, then sends three segments. The second segment is lost. The server acknowledges only contiguous data, and the client resends the missing segment after its retransmission timer expires.

<TcpExchange />

An acknowledgement only advances when the receiver has a contiguous prefix of the stream. A later arrival cannot fill an earlier gap. Read the repeated acknowledgements alongside the missing segment, then follow the retransmission to see why several received segments can be acknowledged together.

This is a simplified exchange: segment numbers identify the illustrated chunks rather than TCP byte offsets, and the drawing uses explicit message timings rather than a complete congestion-control simulation.

`gap` is the interval between consecutive send times. Without it, a message departs when the previous message arrives. `latency` is travel time and defaults to 1. Choose any consistent unit; the timeline scales to fit the figure's height and animation duration.
