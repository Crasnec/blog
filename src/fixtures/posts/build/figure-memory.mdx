An allocator chooses a free region for each allocation and returns released blocks to the free list. That choice can produce very different layouts after several allocations and releases.

Both figures send the same ten requests to a heap of thirty-two cells in the same order. Only the rule for choosing a free region changes.

## First-fit: choose the first region that fits

Releasing A leaves a twelve-cell region near the front and a seven-cell region near the back. First-fit places six-cell F in the first region, leaving regions of six and seven cells. The final nine-cell request G cannot fit, despite thirteen free cells in total.

## Best-fit: choose the smallest region that fits

Best-fit chooses the smallest region large enough for a request. F occupies the seven-cell region, preserving the twelve-cell region for G.

<BestFitAllocation />

Best-fit does not always win. Choosing a close fit can leave many fragments too small to use; this example ends with separate regions of three cells and one cell. Results depend on request sizes and order, which is one reason real allocators organise free regions into size classes.
