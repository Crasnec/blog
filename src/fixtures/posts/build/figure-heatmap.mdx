Daily and weekly averages hide when a service becomes slow. A weekday-by-hour grid gives each hour its own cell, with stronger colour for higher latency. Repeated patterns form bands, while an isolated incident stands out as a single cell.

This example shows one week of API p95 latency. Cells appear from hour 0 to hour 23. Once the grid is complete, the three slowest cells receive outlines and value labels.

## Reading the pattern

In this example's synthetic workload, weekday evening bands represent predictable demand peaks. Monday at 09:00 is a separate spike from a scheduled settlement batch sharing the database. That hour barely changes the weekly average but is immediately visible in the heatmap.

The lighter weekend rows reflect the workload's lower traffic and much smaller evening peaks.

Keep the colour scale consistent when comparing weeks. An automatically rescaled quiet week can otherwise look as intense as a severe incident. Also keep the aggregation explicit: a cell here represents an hourly p95, and averaging those percentiles would not recover the p95 of all requests combined.
